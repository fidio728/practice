# How Pharma Firms Hide Negative or Insignificant Results (2018-2026)

## Short answer
Selective non-disclosure is **not just binary**. In practice, three patterns co-exist:

1. **Whole-trial non-disclosure** (classic file drawer).
2. **Partial disclosure within a disclosed trial** (outcome switching, selective safety/subgroup reporting).
3. **Multi-trial cherry-picking** (publish positive stand-alone results, bury negative trials in pooled/secondary analyses).

## Real-world evidence

### 1) Whole-trial non-disclosure
- Cochrane review (2024, 204 studies, 165,135 trials): about **47% remained unpublished**; positive trials were published more often and faster.
  - https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/MR000011_linchuangshiyannojieguokagongbiaosarerumatenoqijianha
- Lancet cohort (analysis of trials due 2018-2019): only **40.9%** reported within 1 year; **36.2%** had no results available at analysis time.
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31958402/

### 2) Partial reporting inside one disclosed trial
- 2020 phase III analysis: **74%** of trials had at least one discrepancy between ClinicalTrials.gov and publication.
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32703252/
- 2019 BMJ Open analysis: only **35.5%** of included RCTs posted registry results; safety reporting differences could alter comparative rankings.
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31492792/
- SEC enforcement example (Sept 26, 2024): Cassava case alleging selective highlighting of subset findings while omitting full-set null cognition finding.
  - https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-151

### 3) Multi-trial selective framing
- Antidepressant analysis (2018/2019): negative trials were much more likely to appear only in pooled publications (**41.1%** vs **3.8%** for positive trials).
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30261934/
- Newer antidepressants update (2022): transparent publication **100%** for positive vs **47%** for negative trials.
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35045113/

## How regulators reacted

### United States
1. **Seife v. HHS** (Feb 24, 2020): narrowed a disclosure loophole and expanded practical reporting obligations for older trials.
   - https://law.yale.edu/mfia/projects/open-data/seife-v-hhs
2. **FDA notices of noncompliance**:
   - first company notice: **April 27, 2021** (Acceleron)
   - first individual investigator notice: **August 31, 2021**
   - FDA table current to **Feb 5, 2026** shows additional actions in 2022-2025.
   - https://www.fda.gov/science-research/fdas-role-clinicaltrialsgov-information/clinicaltrialsgov-notices-noncompliance-and-civil-money-penalty-actions
3. **FDA transparency commentary**: agency reports many pre-notices; most corrected after warning.
   - https://www.fda.gov/news-events/fda-voices/importance-clinical-trial-transparency-and-fda-oversight
4. **HHS OIG audit** (Aug 12, 2022): NIH oversight gaps in timely reporting.
   - https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2022/the-national-institutes-of-health-did-not-ensure-that-all-clinical-trial-results-were-reported-in-accordance-with-federal-requirements/

### European Union
1. Clinical Trials Regulation (EU No 536/2014) became applicable **Jan 31, 2022**.
2. CTIS single entry from **Jan 31, 2023**.
3. Revised transparency rules from **Jun 18, 2024**.
4. Full applicability from **Jan 31, 2025**.
- https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/clinical-trials/clinical-trials-regulation-eu-no-5362014_en
- https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/clinical-trials-regulation-becomes-fully-applicable

## After regulation: do firms still hide?
Yes, but behavior shifts.

- **Inference from the evidence above**: strict non-reporting is under more pressure due to registry requirements and enforcement, but selective interpretation, delayed reporting, and partial outcome/safety disclosure remain persistent risks.
