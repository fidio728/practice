# How Pharmaceutical Companies Hide Negative or Insignificant Trial Results

> Focus: Real-world evidence, news, journalism, interviews, and investigations (2018–2026).
> Each claim is followed by a reference or link.

---

## 1. The Core Problem: What "Hiding" Means in Practice

The problem is commonly called **publication bias** or the **file drawer problem** — negative or null results are systematically less likely to reach the public than positive ones. But hiding is not one thing. It takes at least **three distinct forms**:

---

### Form A: The Whole Trial Never Gets Published (Binary Non-Disclosure)

The most blunt form. A sponsor runs a trial, sees unflattering results, and simply never submits it for publication. The trial exists on a registry (or not at all), but the results vanish.

- A landmark 2008 study of FDA-registered antidepressant trials found that **97% of positive trials were published**, but only **39% of negative or questionable trials** were. Of the unpublished trials, most simply disappeared — but 11 were published anyway, reframed to look positive.
  > [Selective publication of antidepressant trials and its influence on apparent efficacy — PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8769343/)

- A 2022 replication study found the pattern persisted with newer antidepressants: transparent publication occurred in **100% of positive trials** but only **47% of negative ones**.
  > [Review reports improved transparency in antidepressant drug trials — ScienceDaily, Jan 2022](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/01/220119142832.htm)

- A *Science*/AAAS investigation checked **4,768 trials** on ClinicalTrials.gov and found sponsors violated the reporting law **more than 55% of the time**.
  > [FDA and NIH let clinical trial sponsors keep results secret and break the law — Science/AAAS, 2020](https://www.science.org/content/article/fda-and-nih-let-clinical-trial-sponsors-keep-results-secret-and-break-law)

- The NYU Langone study found almost half of all reviewed drugs had **at least one undisclosed Phase II or III trial**, with only 65% of trials published or reported in any meaningful way.
  > [Big Pharma Inconsistent with Disclosure — NYU Langone News](https://nyulangone.org/news/big-pharma-inconsistent-disclosure-information-clinical-trials-new-study-finds)

---

### Form B: One Trial Is Run, But Only Partial Results Are Disclosed (Selective Outcome Reporting / Outcome Switching)

A sponsor runs a single trial but registers it with one primary endpoint, then — after seeing the data — reports a different endpoint that looks better, or simply drops unfavorable secondary outcomes.

- The **COMPare Project** (led by Ben Goldacre, Oxford) prospectively audited every trial published in the top 5 medical journals between Oct 2015–Jan 2016. Of 67 trials: only **9 were perfect**. The other 58 had **301 outcomes silently dropped** and **357 new outcomes silently added**.
  > [COMPare: correcting and monitoring 58 misreported trials in real time — Trials Journal, 2019](https://trialsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13063-019-3173-2)

- A 2021 meta-research study found **outcome switching occurred in 40% of follow-up publications** of previously published RCTs, and most switches were neither pre-specified nor explained.
  > [High incidence of outcome switching in follow-up publications — Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, 2021](https://www.jclinepi.com/article/S0895-4356(21)00146-3/fulltext)

- A specific documented tactic: **HARKing** (Hypothesizing After Results are Known) — authors add statistically significant outcomes they measured but did not pre-specify as primary.
  > [Is Outcome Switching Still A Problem In Clinical Trials? — Clinical Leader](https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/is-outcome-switching-still-a-problem-in-clinical-trials-0001)

- Ben Goldacre's live commentary on a high-profile COVID-era outcome switch:
  > [Ben Goldacre tweet thread on outcome switching — Twitter/X, May 2020](https://x.com/bengoldacre/status/1256548400390008832?lang=en)

- The COMPare live tracking tool is still active:
  > [compare-trials.org — ongoing](https://www.compare-trials.org/)

---

### Form C: Multiple Trials Are Run, But Only the Positive One Is Published (Portfolio Cherry-Picking)

A sponsor runs several trials, waits to see which produces positive results, then publishes only those. The others are buried.

- The reboxetine case is the canonical example: **7 trials were run**; only **1 (the positive one)** was published. Doctors prescribed it based on that one trial. When all data were finally pooled, the drug was found no better than placebo.
  > [Trial sans Error: How Pharma-Funded Research Cherry-Picks Positive Results — Scientific American (excerpt from Ben Goldacre's *Bad Pharma*)](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/trial-sans-error-how-pharma-funded-research-cherry-picks-positive-results/)

- A more recent structural version: pharma companies **pool negative trials together** so each individual pooled trial looks inconclusive rather than clearly negative — discovered in FDA-registered antidepressant trials.
  > [Hiding negative trials by pooling them — PMC, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6712952/)

- Industry-sponsored trials show **positive results 85% of the time**; independently funded trials do so only **50% of the time** — a gap too large to be explained by random chance.
  > [How Drug Company Money Is Undermining Science — Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-drug-company-money-undermining-science/)

- A working paper covering 2007–2019 found that approval of a competitor's drug **reduces by ~13% the likelihood** that competing trials will report within 2 years. Strategic silence is the norm in competitive markets.
  > [Why Pharma Firms Withhold Trial Results Despite Regulation — HEC Paris](https://www.hec.edu/en/dare/strategy-leadership/why-pharma-firms-withhold-trial-results-despite-regulation)
  > [What Influences Pharma Companies to Disclose? — HEC Paris](https://www.hec.edu/en/what-influences-pharmaceutical-companies-disclose-clinical-trial-results-or-not)

## 2. Historical High-Stakes Example

The **Vioxx scandal** (Merck) is the most cited real-world catastrophe caused by hiding negative data. The NEJM published a Merck-authored study; editors later admitted they had not seen data showing **3 additional heart attacks** — which were omitted. Had full data been disclosed, the drug's cardiovascular risk (5× higher heart attack rate) would have been apparent years earlier. Estimated US deaths: ~30,000.

> [Big Pharma & Clinical Trials — DrugWatch (overview)](https://www.drugwatch.com/featured/clinical-trials-and-hidden-data/)

## 3. Regulatory Response: What Rules Were Introduced

### United States — FDAAA (2007) and the 2017 Final Rule

- The **FDA Amendments Act (FDAAA) of 2007** required Phase II/III trial sponsors to register on ClinicalTrials.gov and post results within **12 months of trial completion**. Non-compliance: up to **$10,000/day fine**.
- In 2017, the **Final Rule** expanded coverage to include most clinical trials of FDA-regulated products, even pre-approval.
- Congress's intent was driven directly by Vioxx and Paxil (hidden pediatric suicide data).

> [Registration, results reporting, and publication bias before/after FDAAA — Trials Journal, 2018](https://trialsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13063-018-2957-0)

### European Union — EUCTR and the 2022 Clinical Trials Regulation

- The **EU Clinical Trials Register (EUCTR)** required results posting within 12 months. The **Clinical Trials Regulation (EU No 536/2014)** came into full effect on **31 January 2022**, with the new CTIS portal becoming mandatory.
- From **January 2022**, national EU regulators gained the power to issue fines to non-compliant sponsors.
- The EMA endorsed new CTIS transparency rules that took effect **June 2024**.

> [European law could improve 'scandalous' lack of clinical trial data reporting — Science/AAAS](https://www.science.org/content/article/european-law-could-improve-scandalous-lack-clinical-trial-data-reporting)

## 4. How Regulators Actually Enforced the Rules (Spoiler: Mostly They Didn't)

### FDA: Rules Without Teeth

- In **7 years after FDAAA became mandatory**, the FDA levied **zero fines** despite thousands of violations.
- By 2021, the FDA had sent notices of noncompliance to only **4 of ~15,000 sponsors** with completed trials since January 2018. Total fines collected: **$0**.
- In **April 2021**, the FDA issued its **first-ever citation** for an FDAAA violation — to a single small institution.
  > [In a first, FDA cites violation of clinical trials reporting law — Science/AAAS, 2021](https://www.science.org/content/article/first-fda-cites-violation-clinical-trials-reporting-law)

- In **February 2024**, the FDA told an advocacy group it is **not planning to get tougher**, citing resource constraints.
  > [FDA gives a mixed response to a petition seeking greater transparency — STAT News, Feb 2024](https://www.statnews.com/pharmalot/2024/02/22/fda-clinical-trials-transparency-medicines/)

- In **September 2023**, the FDA sent a letter to one organization demanding overdue trial data — results were still not submitted after "extensive correspondence." This may signal a new enforcement posture, but has not yet produced fines.
  > [ClinicalTrials.gov: Recent Action May Signal Increased Attention — Morgan Lewis, Jan 2025](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/asprescribed/2025/01/clinicaltrialsgov-recent-action-may-signal-increased-attention-from-fda-on-trial-data-reporting-compliance)

- Advocates describe the situation bluntly: "Leading research entities routinely and flagrantly ignored their obligations."
  > [AllTrials.net — FDAAA enforcement update](https://www.alltrials.net/news/fdaaa-enforcement-clinical-trials-fda-nih/)

### EMA / EUCTR: Better Enforcement Patchy Across Countries

- Since the EU Trials Tracker launched in 2018, timely reporting rose from **50% to 72%** — real but incomplete progress.
- The EMA sent **~30,000 reminders** to overdue sponsors since 2018.
- As of the most recent TranspariMED data: **3,846 completed trials (~28% of the total)** still had no posted results on EUCTR.
- **Italy alone**: ~1,299 missing results. France: hundreds missing, with the largest European clinical trial center (Paris AP-HP) having reported results for **1 of 35 completed trials**.
- Countries with proactive national regulators (Germany, Austria, Denmark, Belgium) improved substantially; others (Italy, France, Spain) have barely moved.
  > [TranspariMED Reports — ongoing](https://www.transparimed.org/reports)
  > [Clinical Trial Transparency in France — TranspariMED, 2021](https://transparency-france.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Clinical-Trial-Transprency-in-France-CONFIDENTIAL-20210206.pdf)

## 5. Are They Still Hiding After Regulation? Yes, But It Is Complicated

### The honest answer is: **yes, hiding persists, but its form has shifted**.

**What improved:**
- Among *newly approved* antidepressants (post-FDAAA), the share of transparent negative trial publication rose from 11% to 47%.
  > [ScienceDaily, Jan 2022](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/01/220119142832.htm)
- Positive results after FDAAA: 0% unpublished (down from some being unpublished before).
- Large pharma companies are somewhat more compliant than academic centers or small sponsors, likely because regulatory risk is more salient for them.

**What has not improved:**
- Outcome switching persists, is largely undetected, and journal editors rarely correct it.
  > [Is Outcome Switching Still A Problem? — Clinical Leader](https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/is-outcome-switching-still-a-problem-in-clinical-trials-0001)
- Academic hospitals and universities — not pharma companies — are now the *largest* source of unreported EUCTR results.
  > [TranspariMED](https://www.transparimed.org/reports)
- Mandated disclosure is especially ignored when a **competitor drug succeeds**: pharma companies suppress results of losing candidates to avoid helping rivals. This is a purely strategic, post-regulation behavior.
  > [Mandated Disclosure Ignored — UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/mandated-disclosure-of-clinical-trials-ignored-especially-for-competitive-drugs/)
- Transparency may actually **reduce willingness to run novel trials**: one 2023 study found large pharma firms cut innovative trial investment after transparency mandates, preferring incremental drugs where data control is easier.
  > [Does Transparency Hinder Technological Novelty? — MSOM / INFORMS, 2023](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2023.0527)
- COVID-era trial misconduct: a widely discussed COVID treatment trial was found to have silently switched its primary endpoint mid-trial — and regulators took no action.
  > [Ben Goldacre tweet, May 2020](https://x.com/bengoldacre/status/1256548400390008852)

## 7. Summary Table: Three Types of Hiding

| Type | Mechanism | Still Happening? | Regulation Addressed It? |
|------|-----------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| **Whole trial non-publication** | Register trial, never report results | Yes — 28% of EU trials, 55%+ US violations | Partially, for big pharma; not for academia |
| **Partial results / outcome switching** | Change or drop endpoints after seeing data | Yes — 40% of follow-up RCTs | Mostly no; journals rarely enforce |
| **Multiple trials, publish only winner** | Run several trials, bury the losers | Yes — especially for me-too drugs | No — legally unclear, almost never prosecuted |
